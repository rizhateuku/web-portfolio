Finishing the unfinished: GoFood Gifting, Ramadan 2025.

Role Senior Product Designer
Team GoFood, Gojek
Timeline Q4 2024 · ~4 months
Status Live · All-time high
+68%
+68% YoY uplift in checkout contribution from Gifting vs Ramadan 2024
+47% YoY uplift in MTU contribution from Gifting vs Ramadan 2024
+29% YoY increase in feature adoption at checkout — driven by the contextual nudge
Context

Ramadan isn't a soft bump. It's the year.

Ramadan in Indonesia isn't a soft seasonal bump. It's the single largest commercial moment of the year. For a food delivery platform, planning starts months ahead — limited-edition SKUs, merchant onboarding, theming, marketing.

Heading into Ramadan 2025, the team was firmly in execution mode on the supply side. What we noticed, talking to each other one week, was that none of it addressed gifting — even though gifting (hampers, parcels, food sent to family and colleagues you can't visit) is one of the most distinctive behaviors of the season.

GoFood had a gifting feature. It was a single nudge at checkout that asked: "Is this order for someone else?" That was it. Adoption reflected the investment.

The opportunity

Not a year-round bet. A seasonal lever.

I sat down with my PM and researcher to scope what we could realistically do in four months. The frame we landed on: gifting as a seasonal conversion lever — not a long-term product bet, but a feature that, properly built up, could absorb a real share of Ramadan demand and pay back its design and engineering cost in a single quarter.

The question wasn't "what's the gifting product we want long-term?" It was "what's the smallest set of additions that lets gifting carry its weight during this Ramadan?"

The shelved research

Two-year-old insights that were still accurate.

Mid-conversation, our researcher mentioned something useful: there were post-launch insights from when gifting first shipped two years ago. They'd never been actioned. We pulled them up and sat with them for an afternoon.

The report wasn't out of date. It was full of user voices we hadn't responded to.

From the 2-year-old post-launch report
People setting a delivery address different from their current location were often sending a gift — and we weren't acknowledging that signal anywhere in the flow.
Users wanted ways to find gift-appropriate items, not just flag an order as a gift after the fact.
Personalization — notes, messages — came up repeatedly as something users expected from a gifting experience and didn't find.
Research repository — insights summary
"The cheapest research is research you've already paid for. We had insights two years old that were still accurate — they'd just never been picked up."
Post-launch synthesis, GoFood Gifting
Workshop & prioritization

Two things that made the cut.

I ran a mini workshop with the PM, researcher, and engineering leads. We mapped the old insights against what was buildable in four months and prioritised by impact and effort.

01
Contextual nudge: location-mismatch trigger

When a user sets a delivery address materially different from where they are, we surface a prompt: "Sending this to someone? Mark it as a gift." This is the closest thing to a free signal we had. The old research showed users sending gifts almost always had a location mismatch — and we'd been ignoring it.

Contextual nudge — delivery address ≠ current location
02
Curated Ramadan collection on Home

A dedicated discovery surface on Home featuring hampers, Ramadan parcels, and gift-appropriate SKUs from partner merchants. This addressed the "how do I find gift-worthy items in the first place" gap directly from the two-year-old research.

Home — curated Ramadan collection (hampers, parcels, gifts)
The UT moment that changed the scope

One comment that changed the project.

We mocked the two improvements and ran usability testing before locking design. The functional pieces worked. But one comment from a participant stopped the room.

"
"I think if we can also have a cute card template and a personal messaging, that would add personalization value even more."
UT participant · GoFood Gifting usability test
That comment was the entire emotional core of the feature. Gifting isn't a logistics operation — it's an act of care, and care is communicated through small, deliberate touches. A card template. A personal note. The recipient receiving something that feels like it was sent with thought, not just a delivery receipt.
Card template + personal message designs

I took this back to the team. The honest tension was timeline. Adding card templates and personalized messaging meant additional design, content, and engineering work on an already tight schedule.

I argued for adding them anyway. The nudge and collection would lift adoption. The delighters would lift meaning — and meaning is what makes someone gift again, and tell their family about the feature. Gifting without personalization is just delivery to a different address. Pulling the delighters into v1 would be cheaper now than retrofitting them post-Ramadan when the team had moved on.

We shipped all three.

"Functional improvements drive adoption. Delighters drive return. For a feature about care, the delighters aren't extras — they're the product."
GoFood Gifting · design rationale, Ramadan 2025
The result

An all-time high — from finishing what we'd started.

+68%
YoY uplift in checkout contribution

The lift wasn't from any single intervention. It was from finally giving the feature what it should have had when it first shipped — a way to be discovered, a context-aware prompt, and the emotional surface that made it feel like a gift, not a delivery.

Ramadan 2025 vs Ramadan 2024: all-time high gifting adoption on GoFood.

+47%
YoY uplift in MTU contribution

More monthly transacting users reached through Gifting than any previous Ramadan — driven by the curated Home collection and widened top-of-funnel discovery.

+29%
YoY feature adoption at checkout

Directly attributable to the contextual nudge — the location-mismatch trigger surfaced the gifting option to the right users at exactly the right moment.

Reflection

What I took from this.

01
"Underinvested" is often a euphemism for "unfinished"

When a feature has low adoption, the answer isn't always to start over. Sometimes the original idea was right and the surrounding system was missing. Ask which version of "underinvested" you're dealing with before you scope.

02
Old research is undervalued

Insights two years old were still accurate. The thing that changed wasn't the user — it was the moment that made the insights matter. Researchers and designers should regularly walk through old reports when planning new work. The cheapest research is the research you've already paid for.

03
Delighters earn their place in emotional contexts

I would not advocate for "cute card templates" in most product contexts. In this one, they were the product. Senior judgment is partly knowing which categories of work that conventional wisdom calls "nice to have" are, for this specific feature, actually load-bearing.

04
Tight timelines force the right question

"What's the smallest set of additions that lets this carry its weight this Ramadan?" is a sharper question than "what's our long-term gifting strategy?" — and produced a cleaner outcome.

05
What I'd do differently

I'd push for a small post-Ramadan study to understand which intervention drove the most lift — was it the nudge, the collection, the delighters, or the combination? The +29% on the nudge is clean; the rest is harder to attribute. That instrumentation should have been planned upfront.
